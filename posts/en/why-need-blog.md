### "The Bitterness of Game Release: Why Is Everyone Else Selling But Me?!"

Hello, everyone! I'm a developer who started recording game development logs. In today's first post, I'm going to tell you the 'desperate' reason why I opened this blog.

**[The Full Story]**

I ambitiously released Flutter games I developed on the Google Play Store. 'Coin Millionaire', 'Popping World', 'Flip Flop'... Don't the names sound like they're going to be hits? (No? 😅)

But reality was harsh. The sales graph maintained a flat line like a cardiac arrest patient's heart rate graph. Yes, my game didn't sell at all. The number of downloads could be counted on one hand.

**"I thought if I uploaded it to the Google Play Store, everyone would download it automatically?!"**

### Recognizing the Problem: No One Knows My Game!

I faced a serious problem. No matter how hard I worked on making the game, my game was smaller than dust among the countless games that exist in the world. Even if I typed the game name in the Google Play Store search bar, it was a game only I knew.

Eventually, I realized. "I need a window to let people know about my game!"

### Opening Promotion Channels: Starting YouTube and X

To spread the charm of my game to the world, I opened two channels.

1. **YouTube:** I plan to upload game introduction videos and devlogs containing the development process.
2. **X (formerly Twitter):** I plan to briefly share game progress or small development episodes and communicate.

### Creating My Own Hideout, A Blog!

But I needed a 'hideout' that would be the center of all these channels. A hub where content is gathered in one place and people can easily find it. I thought about platforms like Naver Blog or Tistory, but as a web developer, my pride somehow didn't allow it. (I make my own space!)

I also considered WordPress, which is often used for opening blogs, but I didn't like studying plugins and fitting into a set frame. It didn't fit my free-spirited development style.

### "If you're a web developer, you should build your own web page!"

So I decided to build this blog myself. A space of my own where I record my game development logs, share the games I made, and communicate with the world.

In the next post, I will share in detail what technology stack I used to build this blog and why I chose those technologies. Look forward to my tumultuous blog building story!
