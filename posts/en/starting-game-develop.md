Everyone, deep in my heart, a hot flame for games was always burning. One day, while living coolly as a full-stack developer, this thought suddenly crossed my mind. "What if I make a game with this cool technology?" I, who had only enjoyed games until now and never thought of making one, suddenly gained confidence that I could do it. So, I bravely stepped into the world of 'Hobby-Job Alignment'.

### Unity, Even You Betray Me...

Embracing the dream of game development, the first place I ran to was Unity. Since it's such a famous engine, I started without much doubt. "This should be a piece of cake!" I said, making a simple casual mobile game. The game was a very simple one where the game scene and clear scene were loaded alternately, and the amount and speed of objects in the game scene gradually increased. But a problem occurred.

Stage 1: Joyful game development. "Wow, the game I made is moving! It's amazing!"
Stage 10: Detecting an ominous vibe. "Uh? It seems to be getting a little slow?"
Stage 15: Sudden death. "Argh! Why is it stuttering like this?! My game can't be a crappy game!"

As the stages went up, the game performance dropped uncontrollably. With stuttering frames and delays, I had nightmares every night. 'Check memory leaks', 'Optimize draw calls', 'Run profiler', etc... I went through all the hardships that Unity developers go through, but there were no significant results.

Of course, if I asked the community, the experts would have given me answers... but for some reason, I didn't want to reach out. Was I afraid of being caught for my incompetence? Or was I already exhausted? Eventually, I knelt down and decided to leave to find a new game engine.

### The Alternative is a Light and Agile Engine

So I started wandering around looking for a game engine that was 'cross-platform', 'optimized for mobile', and 'lightweight and controllable with code'. Suddenly, the **Godot Engine**, with a name that looked powerful like 'God of War', caught my eye.
But as soon as I turned on the editor, I was once again engulfed in fear at the layout of the editor that looked familiar. 'Am I going to get exhausted just studying the engine again like when I was with Unity?' Eventually, I shook my head and started looking for another engine.

### Flutter Geek Meets Flame Engine!

While snooping around various engines, a familiar name suddenly came to mind. During a bootcamp assignment, there was a task to create an app with a new language, and what I chose then was **Flutter** (the language is Dart, Flutter is not a language but a framework), and **Flutter** became my favorite since then. 'No way, can I make a game with Flutter?' I started searching with doubt.
And like destiny, I met the **Flame Engine**. A game engine running on top of Flutter! It completely captivated my heart in that there is no separate editor, it can be controlled by code, and it is lightweight and has excellent performance.
In the next post, I plan to dig into the Flame Engine and reveal the games I made myself. Please look forward to what kind of games a Flutter geek made after meeting the Flame Engine!
